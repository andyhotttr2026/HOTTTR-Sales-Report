Progress

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated. They describe the calendar week beginning 17 Aug.

What actually got done — and the results

  • Average unlock price reached its highest level of the month, with PPV rising to over 80% of revenue.
  • Renewal rate improved week on week — retention is strengthening even as new intake softens.
  • Second-tier creators grew, broadening revenue away from the top account.
  • Two new creators onboarded, both already producing revenue.
  • Daily and per-shift revenue reporting now generates and publishes automatically.

What did not get done — and the effect

  • Acquisition was not addressed. New subs per day fell sharply while revenue held, because monetisation absorbed the shortfall. We are extracting more from a base we are refilling more slowly, with no diagnosis for why intake fell.
  • Creator history exports were not made routine. When a creator leaves, their entire earnings history becomes unretrievable from Infloww. One late-August departure was not exported in time and that history is permanently gone.
  • Ongoing per-person conversion measurement was not built, in a business where PPV is over 80% of revenue.

#1 focus for the upcoming week

Diagnose and begin fixing the acquisition decline.

Why: New subs per day are down sharply while revenue is up. That is a good problem for one month and a serious one for three. We currently cannot diagnose it — traffic source and promotional spend are not tracked anywhere, so we cannot distinguish a promo ending, an algorithm change, reduced posting cadence, or seasonality.

What it brings: Restoring the earlier intake rate would mean roughly 35 additional new subscribers per day, on the order of $2,000 per day of recoverable run rate. Instrumenting traffic sources is the prerequisite — without it, any fix is guesswork.

Problems & input needed

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated.

Biggest blocker

Blocker
We have a revenue dashboard, not a business dashboard. We can see net revenue, subscribers, unlock prices and per-creator splits to the penny. We have no promotional spend, no traffic source, no cost per acquisition, no chatter payroll and no agency split in the same view. We can answer 'did we move volume' but not 'did we make money' or 'where should the next dollar go'.
If we had a magic wand
Instrument acquisition end to end — know where every new subscriber came from and what it cost. The largest movement in our month is a sharp fall in new subscribers, and it is undiagnosable with the data we hold.

Where we are stuck or unsure

  • Chatter quality spread. A three-day sample showed unlock rates ranging from roughly 6% to nearly 60% across the team. In a business where PPV is over 80% of revenue that spread should be worth a great deal, but three days is not enough to size it or manage anyone on it.
  • Top creator softening. She is over half of revenue and down week on week. We cannot tell from the data whether a small number of high-value fans went quiet or whether outbound volume dropped. The two have very different fixes.
  • Staff and creator churn. Several creators left within a short window, and on the chatting side the median tenure of leavers is around two weeks. We suspect a systemic onboarding and ramp problem rather than individual fit, but we have no exit data to confirm it.
