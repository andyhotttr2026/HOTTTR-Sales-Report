Progress

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated. They describe the calendar week beginning 31 Aug.

What actually got done — and the results

  • Built per-chatter performance measurement for the first time. We can now see PPVs sent, unlock rate, fans chatted, message volume and response time for every chatter, per creator, per day. This was our stated blocker last week and it is now closed.
  • Used it immediately to locate the largest single movement in the business: one chatter's output on our top creator fell $6,115 week on week, driven by fewer and lower-priced offers rather than absence. Message volume and fans contacted both rose over the same period.
  • Second-tier creators grew again. Fox Heart +90% and Ella Robinson +35% week on week, together adding $3,543.
  • Automated the reporting itself. Daily, per-shift and this weekly check-in now generate and publish without manual work.

What did not get done — and the effect

  • Acquisition still not addressed. New subscribers fell 38% week on week, from 582 to 361, while renewals fell 6% and the average unlock stayed flat at $50.33. Revenue held up because monetisation absorbed it. We still cannot diagnose the cause because traffic source and promotional spend are not recorded anywhere.
  • No standard for how PPV offers are made. We can now measure that unlock rates range from 16% to 73% across the team, but there is no documented approach for anyone to be measured against or trained toward.
  • Creator history exports still not routine. When a creator leaves, their entire earnings history disappears from Infloww. One departure in late August was not exported in time and is permanently gone.
  • Chatter onboarding was not addressed. Median tenure of leavers is around two weeks and 45 of 46 departures lasted under 90 days. The consultant has confirmed this is an onboarding failure rather than a hiring or role problem, and pointed to a gated Learn-Test-Apply-Advance structure as the fix. Nothing has been built yet.

#1 focus for the upcoming week

Turn the new chatter data into a standard, starting with the accounts where the money actually moves.

Why: Until this week we suspected chatter consistency was our largest controllable variable but could not prove it. We can now: unlock rates run from 16% to 73%, and a single chatter's change on a single creator accounted for roughly twice the entire agency's week-on-week decline. Unlike acquisition, this is measurable today and needs no new instrumentation.

What it brings: The team sent 822 PPVs and unlocked 464 in the current week, a 56% blended rate. Moving the bottom half of the team toward the top half's conversion is worth more than any pricing change, and we can now measure whether it is working week by week.

Problems & input needed

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated.

Biggest blocker

Blocker
We still have a revenue dashboard, not a business dashboard. The chatting side is now instrumented, but we have no promotional spend, no traffic source, no cost per acquisition, no chatter payroll and no agency split in the same view. We can answer 'did we move volume' and now 'who moved it', but still not 'did we make money' or 'where should the next dollar go'.
If we had a magic wand
Instrument acquisition end to end — know where every new subscriber came from and what it cost. New subscribers fell 38% this week and it remains the single largest movement we cannot explain.

Where we are stuck or unsure

  • Acquisition cause. New subs fell 38% in one week with no matching drop in conversion or pricing. We cannot tell whether it is a promo ending, an algorithm change, posting cadence, seasonality, or the roster changes from creators leaving. Nothing in our data distinguishes them.
  • Chatter retention. We know the median leaver lasts about two weeks and we now know that is not normal, but we have no exit data, so we cannot tell which part of weeks one and two loses people. Until we can, any onboarding rebuild is designed blind.
  • Whether the chatter spread is skill, assignment, or fan mix. We can now measure that unlock rates vary 4.5x across the team, but not why. A chatter working a creator with a strong whale base will look better than one working a colder audience, and we cannot yet separate the operator from the account.
  • Concentration risk on our top creator. She is 45% of net revenue and accounted for more than the entire agency decline this week. We do not have a plan that reduces this dependency rather than just monitoring it.
