Progress

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated. They describe the same window as the figures above, 4 Sep – 10 Sep.

What actually got done — and the results

  • Diagnosed this week's decline precisely rather than by assertion. It is not acquisition. New subscribers rose 5.3%, paying fans fell 1.4% and subscription revenue was flat. The entire $15,788 fall is PPV, and it splits into 19% fewer unlocks and a 27.4% lower average unlock price.
  • Confirmed it from an independent second source. Per-chatter reporting shows PPVs sent down 12%, unlock rate down from 55.0% to 47.7%, and average sale down 26.5%. Transactions and chatter reporting agree on the price move to within a percentage point.
  • Measured what winning a lapsed fan back is actually worth, from real transactions rather than lifetime value: $69 median and $198 mean net over the 30 days after they return. That sizes a win-back programme at $1,000 to $2,000 a month, not the $130,000 a lifetime-value view implies.
  • Obtained fan-level retention data for the first time. Return odds fall from 30.2% to 10.4% between day 30 and day 45 of silence, measured across 36,056 observed silences.
  • Cohort quality is now measurable. Fans acquired in May are worth $37.92 by month three; fans acquired in June are worth $56.49. May was our largest intake and our worst, which is the first hard evidence we have on where acquisition spend should not go.

What did not get done — and the effect

  • Chatter onboarding still not built. This has been the named fix for three consecutive weeks and nothing has been designed. Median tenure of leavers remains around two weeks.
  • No PPV pricing standard. The average unlock fell 27% in seven days and there is no floor, no owner and no documented approach to measure that against.
  • Costs still not recorded anywhere, so we still have no margin and no cost per acquisition.
  • Creator history exports still not routine. When a creator departs their earnings history is deleted from Infloww permanently, and we have already lost one month's worth.

#1 focus for the upcoming week

Hold the unlock price. It fell from $55.68 to $40.41 in a single week.

Why: Price per unlock is now the largest single driver of revenue and the fastest moving. Volume fell 19% this week; price fell 27%. Unlike acquisition it needs no new instrumentation, and unlike the roster it can be measured daily, by chatter, starting immediately.

What it brings: Two chatters improved conversion this week, to 75.0% and 55.7%, while the previous week's top three each fell more than 60%. If that spread is coachable it is the largest controllable number in the business.

Problems & input needed

Written sections — entered by the team, not generated.

Biggest blocker

Blocker
We still have a revenue view rather than a business view, and this week the gap became sharper rather than smaller. We evaluated an agency dashboard that already has fields for chatter payroll, creator splits and ad spend, and every one of them is zero. What was an instrumentation problem is now a data-entry problem, which means the constraint is ours rather than the tooling's.
If we had a magic wand
Know whether the unlock-rate spread is skill or account. It runs from 18.8% to 75.0% across the team. If it is skill, it is the biggest coachable number we have. If it is the account, we are ranking people on their allocation and every conclusion we draw from it is wrong.

Where we are stuck or unsure

  • Whether the price collapse is deliberate discounting to close sales or unmanaged drift. We keep no pricing log, so we cannot distinguish a decision from an accident.
  • Two consecutive weeks of opposite failures. Last week acquisition fell 38% while monetisation held; this week monetisation fell 41% while acquisition held. We cannot tell whether the team is trading one against the other or whether it is coincidence.
  • Chatter retention. We know the median leaver lasts about two weeks and that this is not normal, but we still have no exit data, so any onboarding rebuild is designed blind.
  • Concentration on our top creator. She is 43.3% of net revenue and accounted for 68.3% of this week's entire decline. We still monitor this rather than address it.
